## Violence on Google Street View

More than ten years before my child was murdered, back when I was married, I had another child.\
The condition of the divorce was that I would never see the child again. For about a month after the divorce, the violence of the shock numbed me, and my brain refused to accept the fact that my child was gone.

A month later, I lost my mind completely.

"Run."

"This way."

"Turn left there."

"Become homeless."

"Pick up trash in that park."

"If you stay there, you'll get to see your child."

I heard voices like these, and the people passing by on the street told me where to go. In the middle of the night, I ran about 30 km from central Osaka and ended up in a town near the sea, where I lived as a homeless man.

One day the police took me into protective custody. In questioning, all I said were crazy things like "I came here on orders" and "the answer is here," so they sent me to a psychiatric hospital. The diagnosis was schizophrenia. I was committed involuntarily, but I escaped. I went home and immediately jumped from the apartment building in a suicide attempt. I couldn't even die, though. All I lost was the first joint of my right thumb.

I grew up unloved, through domestic violence, abuse, and bullying. So I was bewildered by this overwhelming being, a child who loved me unconditionally, without any noise. I was also bewildered that I, who never opened my heart to anyone, loved that child just as cleanly, without any noise. When I was with my child, I discovered for the first time in my life a self that was laughing purely, from the bottom of my heart. And faced with that overwhelming "love," I felt a vague fear that someday I might end up killing this child.

The park where we used to play, the alleys in our neighborhood, the supermarket, the mountains of Minoh we often visited together, the area around the nursery school... Even now, I find myself spending hours on Google Street View, going around those places, searching for my child.
