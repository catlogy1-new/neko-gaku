## Yeah. This is another piece of rubbish, too.

I did meth for 18 years, but not the way most people do. I only used it once every six months, or even less often. A gap of a year was normal.

My way of using meth was to take a lethal dose: specifically, 1 g at a time. If an ordinary person suddenly took that amount, they would almost certainly die.

The moment I took the lethal dose, a foreign substance that surely was never meant to be inside the body would go astray through my blood vessels at a violent speed. As if in response, my identity would sway wildly and show signs of collapse. I have no identity, but I would have the illusion that I did.

An illusion and a collapse of language caused by an excessive discharge of entropy: in other words, the realm of death. In that instant, something like an indescribable terror would hack into my brain while destroying the space of language.

Am I afraid of death? Probably an illusion.

At the same time, an indescribable, infant-like lump, freed from the ego and prior to the ego (this too is probably language, but language that has lost its outward form), would begin to play freely in my brain.

Something like "death" vanished immediately, while something like consciousness remained stubbornly intact.

Before long I could no longer even stand. I would crouch in a corner of a pitch-dark room, cutting my body and head with scissors or a knife, drifting through a world of fantasy that gushed out without end. For three or four days, unable to sleep, eat, or drink, I simply indulged in fantasy.

That scenery was the same as when I was a child, bullied and abused, fantasizing alone. It may have been painful, but for me, it was the safest place.

When the drug wore off and I looked around, something like \<everyday life> was flickering. Through a gap in the curtains of the dark room I could see a little sunlight and hear small birds singing. \<Something like reality> destroyed the fantasy with violent acceleration and whispered its boast that it was \<reality>.

The futon was soaked in blood, and when I walked, it made a squelching sound. The self in the mirror had become gaunt in only about three days, and my whole body was covered in blood. Blood was spurting from the severed vessels, and the vessels were twitching.

A relief that nothing could replace. This was the place I had wanted to come back to.

Mission complete.
